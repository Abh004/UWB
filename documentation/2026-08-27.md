# Engineering & Performance Optimization Log: Dual-Sensor Radar Presence Detection
**Date:** August 27, 2026

---

## 1. Overview & Objectives
Today's session focused on expanding the dual-sensor radar presence detection system to support **5 occupancy states** (`0_people`, `1_people`, `2_people`, `3_people`, `4_people`), resolving environment/CUDA architecture mismatches for the **NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090**, eliminating critical CPU bottlenecks in the training and evaluation pipelines, and diagnosing the low holdout accuracy issue.

---

## 2. Issues Diagnosed & Solutions

### A. PyTorch CPU-Only Fallback
* **Symptom:** Scripts executed with `device = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")` were silently falling back to `cpu`, causing evaluations and training to take extraordinarily long.
* **Root Cause:** The virtual environment (`.venv`) had `torch==2.13.0+cpu` installed by default.
* **Resolution:** Installed PyTorch targeting NVIDIA CUDA wheels.

### B. Blackwell Architecture (`sm_120`) Kernel Incompatibility
* **Symptom:** After installing `torch==2.13.0+cu126`, running training crashed immediately on `Conv2d` with:
  ```text
  torch.AcceleratorError: CUDA error: no kernel image is available for execution on the device
  UserWarning: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 with CUDA capability sm_120 is not compatible with current PyTorch installation.
  ```
* **Root Cause:** The NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 belongs to NVIDIA's Blackwell architecture with **Compute Capability 12.0 (`sm_120`)**. The `cu126` wheel only included pre-compiled kernels up to `sm_90` (Hopper/Ada).
* **Resolution:** Installed PyTorch with native CUDA 13.2 support:
  ```powershell
  pip install torch torchvision --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu132 --force-reinstall
  ```

### C. The DataLoader CPU Bottleneck (GPU Data Starvation)
* **Symptom:** Even after CUDA was active, GPU utilization hovered at ~5% while CPU stayed pinned at 100%. Training 100 epochs was projected to take 40–45 minutes.
* **Root Cause Analysis:**
  The convolutional neural network (`PresenceCNN_X7_Dual`) executes a forward + backward pass in `< 0.3 ms` on an RTX 5090. However, for every batch of 16 samples, the `DataLoader` on the host CPU was executing complex NumPy math:
  1. Generating 2.1+ million complex random floats for Gaussian noise augmentation.
  2. MTI clutter filter mean subtraction across 50 temporal frames.
  3. Complex magnitude calculations across 2 TX $\times$ 2 RX antennas.
  4. Dynamic range compression (`np.log1p`).
  This caused the RTX 5090 to sit idle >95% of the time waiting for the single-threaded CPU DataLoader.
* **Resolution:** Refactored preprocessing into **vectorized PyTorch GPU kernels**:
  * Moved raw complex tensors to CUDA memory once per batch.
  * Implemented `extract_presence_features()` directly in PyTorch GPU operations (`torch.abs`, `torch.mean`, `torch.log1p`).
  * Increased batch size to 64.
* **Performance Gain:** 
  * 50 batches on CPU NumPy: `6.05s`
  * 50 batches on RTX 5090: `0.27s` (**~22x speedup**)
  * Total training time for 40 epochs on 3,440 samples dropped to **~25 seconds**.

### D. Diagnosis of Low Holdout Accuracy (Why It Was Initially at ~30-68%)
* **Analysis:**
  Unlike gesture recognition (which relies on spatial trajectories over time), **human occupancy detection relies fundamentally on total reflected radar energy** (since 2 people reflect $\approx 2\times$ the power of 1 person, 3 people $\approx 3\times$, etc.).
  The previous training augmentation pipeline applied:
  1. **Random Amplitude Scaling ($[0.5, 1.5]$):** Multiplying by $0.5$ cut the radar energy of 2 people down to the exact energy of 1 person.
  2. **Sensor Dropout (15% chance to zero out a whole sensor):** Cutting radar energy in half.
  This completely blurred the energy boundaries between classes, forcing the neural network to confuse 2 people with 1 person and 3 people with 2 people.
* **Fix:** Removed destructive amplitude scaling and sensor zeroing from runtime augmentation, allowing the network to cleanly learn exact radar energy and spatial distribution boundaries.
* **Result:** Real-world holdout accuracy jumped from **68% to 94.00%**!
