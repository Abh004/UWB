## 4. What Limits Accuracy

### 4.1 Retrained on 6 sessions (leave-one-session-out)
Held-out accuracy by session: 64.6 / 92.1 / 89.4 / 76.7 / 79.6 / 92.3%.
Pooled **82.1%** (4 sessions: 77%). The trainer chose confidence 0.90:
precision 95.8%, but only 43.8% of gestures recognised.

### 4.2 Model-side changes (6 sessions, leave-one-session-out)

| variant | accuracy | >= 0.8: precision / recall |
|---|---|---|
| 3 models (current) | 82.1% | 91.6% / 63.1% |
| 5 models | 82.4% | 92.8% / 64.6% |
| 3 models + averaging over time shifts of ±2 and ±4 frames | 82.1% | 91.6% / 58.5% |
| 3 models, 120 epochs | 80.3% | 91.4% / 65.8% |

All four are within about a point of each other, which is noise. The model is
not the limit.

### 4.3 More sessions (learning curve)
Held-out session accuracy against the number of training sessions (1 model):

| training sessions | 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| accuracy | 61.9% | 69.5% | 76.6% | 82.1% |
| >= 0.8: precision | 77.6% | 84.5% | 87.1% | 92.9% |

Each new sitting still adds 2–3 points, and the curve has not levelled off.
This agrees with Sep 29: repetitions within one session stop helping at ~8
per gesture, and variation between sessions is what the model has to learn.

## 5. Seven Sessions and the Margin Floor

### 5.1 Training output
`python x7_gesture_train.py --target-precision 0.9`, with G09301120 added:

```
held out  G09291102 67.7%  G09291116 90.5%  G09291203 85.1%  G09291208 83.7%
          G09301003 79.6%  G09301014 94.2%  G09301120 78.0%
raw accuracy 82.3%
floors conf >= 0.65, margin >= 0.40: precision 90.2%, gesture recall 76.6%
```

| | 6 sessions, target 95% | 7 sessions, target 90% |
|---|---|---|
| accuracy | 82.1% | 82.3% |
| floors | confidence 0.90 | confidence 0.65 + margin 0.40 |
| precision / recall | 95.8% / 43.8% | **90.2% / 76.6%** |

The margin floor reports a gesture only when the top class leads the second
by at least 0.40. It rejects predictions split between two gestures, so the
confidence floor can drop and far more correct gestures get through. At
precision above 90%, recall rose from ~64% to 77%. These floors are stored in
the model's `gesture_v3.json` and used by live by default.

### 5.2 The first session's NO_GESTURE captures
NO_GESTURE captures reported as gestures, at the chosen floors:

| session | reported as a gesture |
|---|---|
| **G09291102** | **12 of 15** |
| all other 6 sessions (including G09301120's 20) | **0 of 64** |

Excluding these 15 captures from scoring, precision is **94.6%** with 76.6%
recall. A retrain with them removed from training gave accuracy 86.2%
against 85.4%, which is noise. So they do not harm training. They are
movements that honestly look like swipes, and they only lower the reported
precision. They were kept.

## 7. Seat Moved 5–10 cm Front or Back

### 7.1 Simulated shift
The range axis of every held-out capture was shifted by whole bins (7.1 cm
each; 7 folds × 3 models):

| seat shift | accuracy | precision | gestures recognised |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14.2 cm closer | 75.1% | 85.5% | 69.2% |
| 7.1 cm closer | 79.1% | 89.4% | 73.6% |
| **none** | **82.5%** | **90.2%** | **76.6%** |
| 7.1 cm back | 83.9% | 90.0% | 74.9% |
| 14.2 cm back | 82.3% | 89.0% | 67.6% |

A 7 cm shift costs little, because training already shifts range by ±1 bin at
random (`augment`). A 14 cm shift costs 7–9 points of recall. Moving closer
costs more than moving back. This simulation moves range only: a hand
farther away also sweeps a smaller angle, which is not modelled.

### 7.2 Real hand distance in the recorded data

| hand at | n | accuracy | precision | recognised |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 0.40 m | 88 | 85.2% | 90.1% | 85.9% |
| 0.40–0.45 m | 97 | 89.7% | 95.3% | 86.2% |
| 0.45–0.50 m | 62 | 77.4% | 82.4% | 76.4% |
| 0.50–0.60 m | 49 | 65.3% | 84.6% | **50.0%** |
| > 0.60 m | 82 | 85.4% | 100% | 52.4% |

0.50–0.60 m is the weakest range; it has the fewest training captures.

### 7.3 Why moving the seat matters less for gestures than for presence
The gesture features subtract each window's mean over time, so static
reflections, including the seat itself, drop out. A seat move affects
gestures only through where the hand ends up. Presence zones depend on the
seat positions themselves, so after a seat move, presence needs `check`.

## 8. SWIPE_RL Sensitivity

Per-gesture recognition at the model's floors (held-out, 7 sessions):

| | LR | RL | UD | DU | PUSH | accuracy | precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| with time reversal (current) | 83% | 72% | 72% | 71% | 85% | 82.5% | 90.2% |
| without time reversal | 77% | 72% | 75% | 69% | 83% | 83.1% | 88.2% |

Time reversal (flipped LR captures used as extra RL training data) is not the
cause: RL is 72% either way.

SWIPE_RL captures that were not reported:

| cause | count |
|---|---|
| read as SWIPE_LR | **9** |
| read as PUSH_IN | 3 |
| read as NO_GESTURE | 2 |
| right class, below the floors | 2 |
| read as SWIPE_UD | 1 |

RL movement energy matches LR's (median moving amplitude 1.4 vs 1.5), so RL
is not too weak to trigger. It is mistaken for the opposite direction.

RL recognised per session, in order: 10/10, 9/10, 8/8, 5/8, 5/8, 6/8, and
**0/8 in G09301120**. The pattern suggests the RL style drifted in the later
sessions (shorter, faster, or starting further to one side). This is inferred
from the numbers, not observed. The current model includes G09301120, so it
has seen that style.

## 9. Procedure After a Seat Change

1. **Gestures, 5–10 cm:** no recalibration.
   `python x7_gesture_live.py --host1 529 --host2 545 --calibrate 0`
2. **For robustness:** record the next sessions at different seat positions
   (e.g. ~10 cm forward, ~10 cm back), with full, deliberate right→left
   swipes, then retrain:
   ```
   python x7_gesture_record.py --host1 529 --host2 545 --per-gesture 8 --no-gesture 20
   python x7_gesture_train.py --target-precision 0.9
   ```
3. **Presence:** `python x7_presence_setup.py check`. It recalibrates only if
   the rows moved (5 cm dead band).

## 12. Lessons

**A frozen model scored on a new day is the best check that nothing moved.**
Static reflections vary almost as much between sessions on the same setup as
between days.

**When every model change is within noise, the model has hit what its data
covers.** More models, test-time averaging and longer training moved nothing;
one extra session with varied non-gesture movements changed the operating
point substantially.

**Accuracy alone can hide a large live improvement.** Accuracy went 82.1% →
82.3%, while recall at 90% precision rose from ~64% to 77%, because the
margin floor separates right from wrong predictions better than confidence
alone.

**Break errors down by session before changing anything.** All non-gesture
false reports came from one session's captures, and SWIPE_RL's weakness came
mostly from one session.
