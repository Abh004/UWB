## 5. Negative-Range Bins — Verified Against Real Data, Then Cropped

`range_offset = -1.38 m` means **bins 0–19 map to negative range** — physically
behind the antenna. They cannot hold a target return; whatever they carry is
TX→RX direct coupling.

Measured on the 56 `cal1` captures (34 bins):

```
 bin    range    raw |x|    MTI |x|   MTI share
   0   -1.38m     0.0004     0.0004       0.0%
  10   -0.67m     0.0458     0.0458       0.2%
  19   -0.02m     0.0879     0.0878       0.4%
  20   +0.05m     0.1225     0.1225       0.6%   <- range zero
  26   +0.48m     2.2757     2.2705      10.3%   <- gesture peak
  33   +0.98m     1.0392     1.0375       4.7%

NEGATIVE (bins 0-19):  MTI 0.0478   4.3% of MTI energy
POSITIVE (bins 20-33): MTI 1.5071  95.7% of MTI energy
ratio neg/pos = 0.03x
```

**This independently validates the corrected range map.** The gesture energy
peaks at **+0.48 m** — natural arm's length. Under the old 6.5 mm assumption the
hand would have sat at 0.17 m and negative range would not exist at all.

**Fixed:** added `x7_physics.FIRST_PHYSICAL_BIN`, *derived* rather than hardcoded:

```python
FIRST_PHYSICAL_BIN = max(0, ceil(-RANGE_OFFSET_M / BIN_LENGTH_M))   # = 20
```

`extract_presence_features` now crops both ends — the zero-padded legacy tail and
the negative-range head. Rationale for dropping them rather than letting the CNN
ignore them: `log1p()` is steepest near zero, so a near-empty bin is amplified
out of all proportion to its information content. This is the same mechanism that
made the fabricated 339-bin padding so damaging.

```
feature tensor:  (50,2,2,2,112) -> (2, 50, 92)     92 bins, +0.05 m .. +6.55 m
batch:         (3,50,2,2,2,112) -> (3, 2, 50, 92)
```

## 6. Window Duration Now Depends on Frame Rate — Documented

`WINDOW_SIZE = 50` was commented "5 seconds at 10 fps". With the recorder's new
5 FPS default, 50 frames is **10 seconds**. The frame count is unchanged, so
nothing breaks, but the comment was misleading and the dependency is now explicit:

```
10 FPS ->  5 s  (1.0-1.7 breath cycles -- too few to establish periodicity)
 5 FPS -> 10 s  (2.0-3.3 cycles)   <- current recorder default
```

Recording and training must agree on frame rate. Recording at 10 FPS and training
with `WINDOW_SIZE = 50` silently halves the observation window.

## 7. The "Only 3 People" Question

### It is not a configuration limit

```
existing presence model : 5 output classes
presence_classes.json   : ['0_people','1_people','2_people','3_people','4_people']
recorder (--seats 4)    : occupancy levels 0..4, all seat combinations
```

Everything already supports 4. There is no cap to remove.

### The actual cause: a stale model with no data behind it

`best_model_2s_presence.pth` dates from Sep 4 and was trained on 112-bin,
old-mounting, 10 FPS data, before the bin-length correction. `PresenceCNN` ends in
`AdaptiveAvgPool2d((1,1))`, so it **accepts** the new 92-bin input without error
and returns meaningless output. Never reaching `4_people` is a symptom of that.

With zero presence recordings on disk, presence detection cannot be run
meaningfully at all until a dataset exists.

### The physics penalty, quantified

Feature is `log1p(power * 1e4)`; reflected power scales ~linearly with occupant
count. A logarithm compresses successive counts:

```
p0 (per person)     N=1    N=2    N=3    N=4  |  gap 1→2  gap 3→4  ratio
          1e-03   2.398  3.045  3.434  3.714  |    0.647    0.280   2.31x
          1e-02   4.615  5.303  5.707  5.994  |    0.688    0.287   2.40x
```

In the saturated regime this tends to `ln(2/1) / ln(4/3)` = **2.41×** —
separating 4 from 3 is inherently 2.41× harder than 2 from 1, before noise or
occlusion. Alternatives by the same measure:

```
log1p(1e4*P)  [current]   2.41x
P**0.25                   1.93x
sqrt(P) = amplitude       1.55x
P  [linear power]         1.00x     uniform spacing
```

**Deliberately not changed.** The log was chosen on Sep 4 for dynamic-range
reasons and it is a genuine trade. All four will be tested on real data once it
exists, and chosen by measurement rather than argument.

### What actually decides it: seat geometry

The range bin is 7.1 cm and the feature preserves full range structure
`(sensors, frames, bins)`, so the CNN can count **peaks** rather than total
energy — which scales past the log limit.

```
seats 30 cm apart in range -> 4.2 bins apart   well resolved
seats 50 cm apart in range -> 7.0 bins apart   very well resolved
```

Two failure modes, both geometric:

* **A row at equal standoff** — occupants share range bins; only angle separates
  them. This is where 4-person counting will fail.
* **Collinear seating** — a body is a near-total blocker at 7–8 GHz, so anyone
  directly behind another is shadowed and contributes far less than their share.
  No feature fixes this.

## 11. Lessons

**A corrected constant must be chased through every consumer.** The 71.4 mm fix
landed in `x7_physics.py` on Sep 10 but three modules kept their own copies, one
of which would have printed a 33 m range. A single source of truth plus imports
would have prevented it; that is now the arrangement.

**Refactors break importers silently.** Renaming `RADAR_FPS` left
`x7_2s_live_presence` unloadable for a full day, and nothing surfaced it because
nothing imported that module during the session. An import check across all
entry-point modules is cheap and was not being done.

**Derive constants, do not hardcode them.** `FIRST_PHYSICAL_BIN` is computed from
the measured offset and bin length, so a future re-measurement carries through
automatically instead of leaving another stale literal.

**Quantify a limitation before trying to fix it.** The 4-person difficulty is real
and now has a number (2.41×) and a mechanism (log compression plus occlusion).
That is enough to act on the geometry — which is free — and to defer the feature
change until data can decide it.
