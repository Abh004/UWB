# Engineering Log — Dual-Sensor X7 Radar
**Date:** September 22, 2026

---

## 1. Session Objective

Review what was outstanding from Sep 21, then answer: *"if there is variance
between each day, what guarantee do I have that it will work in a car?"*

**Outcome:** measurements split the question in two. The instrument is stable
between days (10x less variation than trial noise), so the day-to-day
differences are in the scene, not the sensor. The physics then showed which
cues can generalise: absolute energy cannot (a 20 cm seat slide is -5 dB), and
breathing cannot be resolved per person in a 10 s window. Mid-session the scope
was corrected — the data is from a **room** with car-like seating, for a proof
of concept — which removed most deployment concerns and falsified the Sep 21
"metal cabin multipath" explanation.

The empty-room (0p) set was recorded after the session.

## 3. The Instrument Is Stable

```

09-11     4p        50      0.01770   0.00063   3.5%
09-15     3p       200      0.01781   0.00149   8.4%
09-16     2p       300      0.01754   0.00087   5.0%
09-21     1p       200      0.01766   0.00097   5.5%

between-day spread of means 0.00010   within-day trial sd 0.00099   ratio 0.10
```

Between-day hardware variation is 10x smaller than the variation between
consecutive trials. What differs between days is the scene.

## 4. Seat Position Is a Large Nuisance

```
range bin 71.4 mm      seat travel 10 / 20 / 25 cm = 1.4 / 2.8 / 3.5 bins

front-row person  0.60 -> 0.80 m   -5.0 dB   (R^-4)
                  0.60 -> 0.70 m   -2.7 dB
                  0.60 -> 0.50 m   +3.2 dB
```

Eight of the ten feature channels are unit phasors and therefore
amplitude-invariant; the range translation, however, affects all of them.

## 5. A 10 s Window Cannot Resolve Breathers

```
window 10 s at 5 FPS -> frequency resolution 0.100 Hz
breathing band 0.20-0.33 Hz spans 1.3 FFT bins

rates differing by 0.10 Hz (6.0 /min) need T >= 10 s
                   0.05 Hz (3.0 /min) need T >= 20 s
                   0.02 Hz (1.2 /min) need T >= 50 s
```

## 6. Scope Corrected — Room, Proof of Concept

The user clarified: recordings are made **in a room**, arranged like a car, as a
proof of concept; no engine vibration, sunlight or clothing factors; occupants
are 2 men and 2 women; the target is "almost any car".

This removed seat-travel, vehicle-model and environmental factors from scope.
The bridging session remained the one required item: a PoC's deliverable is a
claim, and day-aliased data supports none.

## 7. Room Geometry — Correcting Sep 21

```
static reflectors      4.26 m (0.071)   5.33 m (0.050)     walls / furniture
moving-energy peak     0.69 m for every occupancy class
moving energy > 2.5 m  1p 16.7%   2p 20.8%   3p 14.5%   4p 16.7%
seat-region energy     1p 0.343   2p 0.327   3p 0.819   4p 0.897
```

The far-field fraction does not rise with occupancy, so it is not occupant
multipath; the "metal cabin" explanation written on Sep 21 was wrong and
`check_confound.py`'s docstring was corrected.

Two further findings:
- **Energy does not separate 1 from 2 people** (0.343 vs 0.327).
- **The back row is ~11 dB weaker** than the front (13x; R^-4 alone predicts 14 dB).

## 11. Lessons

**Measure the instrument separately from the scene.** A ten-second check of the
behind-antenna bins turned "is the radar unreliable?" into a firm no.

**Ask what the data is for before answering robustness questions.** Most of the
first answer was about a deployment the user was not building yet.

**An explanation that was never tested is still a hypothesis.** The cabin
multipath story survived a day because nobody checked whether the far-field
fraction scaled with occupancy.
